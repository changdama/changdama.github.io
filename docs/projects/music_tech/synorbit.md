# SynOrbit

<p class="synorbit-deck">A spatial instrument for composing with time, transforming the flat sequencer timeline into rotating orbital structures that can be seen, heard, and reshaped through gesture.</p>

<figure class="synorbit-hero"><img src="/assets/projects/synorbit/synorbit-hd.jpg" alt="Head-mounted displays prepared for the SynOrbit presentation"><figcaption>SynOrbit presentation setup · GTCMT Seed Grant Presents: Prism Pt. 1</figcaption></figure>

## Sequencing as an orbit

SynOrbit reimagines musical time as a navigable spatial structure. Notes and patterns sit on rotating rings rather than a conventional horizontal timeline: their position around an orbit determines when they sound, while the ring’s rotation makes playback visible as continuous motion.

Hand gestures let performers create, move, and reshape these sequences in real time. The instrument turns rhythmic organization into a physical activity—something that can be reached into, reorganized, layered, and shared during improvisation.

## Presentation and development

SynOrbit was presented on **May 2, 2026** at Georgia Tech’s West Village 175 as part of *GTCMT Seed Grant Presents: Prism Pt. 1 — Sound Through a New Lens*. The ongoing project is supported by the Georgia Tech Center for Music Technology Seed Grant and continues to explore virtual instrument design, temporal interaction, gesture control, and collaborative performance.

Future iterations can extend the observer view into a more expressive performance camera, clarify visual feedback for musical state changes, and study how performers coordinate when multiple orbital timelines are active at once.
